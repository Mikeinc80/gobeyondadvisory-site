Corridors

One corridor. Proven, or not proven.

EKORails is not building a network map. The proposed pilot addresses a single corridor with a defined institutional user group, so that the compliance model, the partner model and the settlement model can be tested and evidenced before anything else is contemplated.

▲ No corridor described on this page is operating. There is no live transaction activity, no active corridor and no launched service.

Why this corridor was selected
Four criteria, documented in the filing: existing documented trade volume between the two markets; the availability of licensed partners on both sides who can perform the conversion, settlement and payout legs; a regulatory pathway that is clear enough to plan against; and a concentration of business payments where the reconciliation problem is acute rather than theoretical. [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS — selection rationale as filed]
Intended institutional users
Licensed banks and payment institutions with corridor obligations; enterprises with recurring, documented trade payments on the corridor; and approved trade counterparties. In a pilot, this is limited to the named participant set agreed with the regulator and partners.
The market friction identified
Payments that arrive without the reference data needed to reconcile them; conversion that depends on currency availability rather than only on price; duplicated compliance checks at each intermediary; and no single participant holding a complete, evidenced record of the transaction. Corridor-specific measurements of these frictions: [INSERT VERIFIED FIGURE — corridor baseline study]
Required partners
At minimum an originating institution, a licensed conversion or liquidity counterparty, a settlement partner and a licensed payout institution in the receiving market — plus identity, screening and monitoring providers. Partner names are published only with written consent, and no partner is described as engaged before an agreement is executed.
Proposed settlement flow
Initiation, identity and compliance validation, transaction authorisation, FX or value conversion by a licensed counterparty, settlement by the responsible licensed institution, then confirmation and audit reporting. See the transaction lifecycle and the system boundary.
Regulatory dependencies
Sandbox participation and its scope; partner authorisations in both markets; data protection obligations in both markets; and any conditions attached to an approval. [SUBJECT TO REGULATORY APPROVAL]
Pilot success measurements
Completeness of the audit record; screening accuracy and false-positive rate; exception and repair rate; reconciliation match rate; adherence to agreed reporting; and time-to-resolution for held transactions. Expansion

What would have to be true before a second corridor.

EKORails does not publish a corridor roadmap, because publishing one would imply commitments that depend on regulators and partners rather than on us. What we can publish is the test a second corridor would have to pass.

Condition 01

The first corridor is evidenced

The pilot has completed, its success measures have been met and the results have been reported to the regulator.

Condition 02

Authorisation exists

The appropriate authorisation or licence is held in each market of the new corridor before any activity begins.

Condition 03

Licensed partners are contracted

Conversion, settlement and payout partners are executed on both sides, not merely in discussion.

Condition 04

Controls transfer

Screening, monitoring and reporting obligations for the new markets are implemented and tested, not assumed.

Condition 05

Demand is documented

Named institutional users with documented corridor obligations, not projected market size.

Condition 06

Operations can carry it

Support, case management and resilience capacity exist for two corridors, not one corridor stretched.

Where we sit

Complementary to existing infrastructure, by design.

A corridor already has participants. Any credible new entrant has to explain what it adds to them rather than what it replaces.

How EKORails is intended to relate to existing systems. Descriptive, not comparative marketing.
SystemWhat it doesIntended relationship to EKORails
PAPSSA pan-African payment and settlement system developed with Afreximbank and African central banks, enabling payments in local currencies between participating institutions.Potentially complementary. Where a corridor is served by PAPSS and a participant is connected, that route may be the right one. EKORails is designed as an orchestration and reconciliation layer, not an alternative settlement system.
SWIFTPrimarily a financial messaging network used by banks to exchange payment instructions. It is not itself clearing, conversion or settlement.Complementary. Messaging remains the way many institutions communicate. EKORails aligns to ISO 20022 semantics so partner banks receive data in a form their systems expect.
Domestic payment switchesNational infrastructure for domestic clearing and payout, with local reach and local cut-offs.Depended upon. The last mile of any corridor runs through domestic rails, reached by a licensed partner in that market.
Central bank settlement systemsReal-time gross settlement and related systems providing settlement in central bank money.Depended upon, through licensed participants. EKORails does not provide settlement finality.
Correspondent bankingBilateral bank relationships used to reach markets where a bank has no direct presence.Coexists. Where a correspondent route is the appropriate route, the value EKORails adds is the record and the controls around it.
