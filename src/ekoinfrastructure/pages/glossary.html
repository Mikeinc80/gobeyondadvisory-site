Glossary.

Terms that get used loosely, defined precisely. Where a term is contested or used differently across jurisdictions, we say so rather than picking the convenient meaning.

ACDFI KLNOP RSTV

A

AML
Anti-money laundering. The body of law, regulation and controls intended to prevent the financial system being used to launder the proceeds of crime.
Authorised dealer
An institution permitted by a central bank to deal in foreign exchange. The term and its scope vary by jurisdiction.

C

CFT
Counter-financing of terrorism. Usually paired with AML in regulation and in a firm’s programme.
Clearing
The transmission, reconciliation and confirmation of payment instructions between institutions, and the calculation of the resulting obligations. Distinct from settlement.
Correspondent banking
An arrangement in which one bank holds an account for another so the second can reach a market where it has no presence.
Corridor
A directional pair of markets between which value moves — for example payments from market A to market B. A corridor has its own participants, rules, costs and frictions, and behaves differently in each direction.
Cut-off time
The time after which an instruction will be processed on the following business day in a given system or market. Cut-offs across time zones are a common and under-appreciated source of delay.

D

De-risking
The withdrawal of correspondent or customer relationships by institutions judging the compliance cost or risk to be too high, rather than in response to a specific finding.
Due diligence
The verification and risk assessment a regulated institution performs on a customer or counterparty, at onboarding and on an ongoing basis.

F

Finality
The point at which a transfer of value becomes irrevocable and unconditional. Defined by system rules and by governing law, not by how quickly a balance updates on a screen.
FX spread
The difference between the rate at which a party will buy a currency and the rate at which it will sell, or between the rate given to a customer and a reference rate. Compensation for holding and pricing currency risk. It can be moved or made visible; it cannot be conjured away.

I

Idempotency
A property of an operation such that repeating it produces no additional effect. Essential in payments, where a retried instruction must never create a second payment.
Interoperability
The ability of separate systems to work together to complete a transaction end to end, including the data, rules and legal arrangements that make that possible — not only a technical connection.
ISO 20022
An international standard for financial messaging that carries structured party, purpose and remittance data. Widely adopted, with staged migration across markets.

K

KYB
Know your business. Verification of a business counterparty: legal existence, ownership, control and the nature of its activity.
KYC
Know your customer. Verification of a customer’s identity and risk profile.

L

Last mile
The final leg of a cross-border payment, in which a licensed institution credits the beneficiary through domestic rails. Frequently where corridor propositions fail.
Liquidity provider
A party willing to hold and exchange currencies at a quoted price and size, taking a position in return for compensation.

N

Netting
Offsetting obligations between parties so that only the net amount is settled. Reduces the value that must move, at the cost of concentrating exposure until settlement occurs.
Nostro / vostro
Two views of the same correspondent account. Nostro is “our account with you”; vostro is “your account with us”.

O

Orchestration
Coordinating the sequence of dependent actions performed by different parties in a transaction, and holding its state until every leg completes or is unwound. Orchestration is not settlement.

P

PAPSS
The Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, developed with Afreximbank and African central banks to enable payments between participating institutions in local currencies. Full explainer.
Payout institution
The licensed institution in the receiving market that credits the beneficiary.
PSP
Payment service provider. A licensing category whose exact scope varies by jurisdiction.

R

Regulatory sandbox
A supervised arrangement permitting a defined test with defined participants, within limits and subject to reporting. Not a licence, and not an endorsement.
RTGS
Real-time gross settlement. A system in which each instruction settles individually and immediately, typically in central bank money, removing netting risk at the cost of higher liquidity requirements.

S

Sanctions screening
Checking parties and payment data against applicable sanctions and watchlists at onboarding and at transaction time.
Settlement
The transfer of value that discharges an obligation between parties. Distinct from messaging, clearing, conversion and payout.
Settlement asset
The instrument in which settlement occurs — central bank money, commercial bank money, or another asset agreed by the parties and permitted by the applicable rules.
Stablecoin
A token intended to hold a constant value against a reference asset, usually a currency. Regulatory treatment differs sharply by jurisdiction and continues to change. A stablecoin does not remove the need for currency conversion at either end of a corridor.
Switch
Domestic infrastructure routing transactions between institutions within a market, often operating the domestic card and transfer schemes.
SWIFT
A financial messaging network used by institutions to exchange standardised instructions. It is not itself clearing, conversion or settlement. Full explainer.

T

Transaction monitoring
Ongoing analysis of activity against expected patterns and rules, raising alerts for review. No monitoring programme detects all illicit activity.
Travel rule
The requirement that specified originator and beneficiary information accompanies a transfer, so that each institution in the chain receives complete data.

V

Velocity limit
A cap on the frequency or cumulative value of transactions over a period, applied per customer, beneficiary or corridor as a control.
