Corridor intelligence

Corridor intelligence.

A corridor is not a market size. It is a set of named participants moving specific value under specific rules, and it can be described precisely if you ask the right questions in the right order.

The method

Most corridor analysis begins with a total addressable market and works down. That produces a number that cannot be acted on. Starting from the participants and working up produces a description that can be tested against reality.

1. Participants

Who actually moves value on this corridor today? Name the banks, the payment institutions, the switches, the aggregators and the informal channels. If a corridor cannot be described in named institutions, the analysis is not yet real.

2. Flows

Separate the flows that get bundled together: trade payments for goods, trade payments for services, intra-group treasury movement, personal remittance, and government or institutional transfers. They have different values, different frequencies, different documentation and different regulatory treatment. A statistic about one is not evidence about another.

3. The cost stack

Cost is rarely a single fee. It is an origination fee, a conversion margin, intermediary charges, a receiving charge, and a set of costs that never appear on an invoice: working capital held against uncertainty, staff time spent reconciling, and delay in releasing goods. The visible fee is often the smaller half.

4. Friction points

Where does a transaction on this corridor actually stop? Common answers include compliance holds on incomplete party data, currency unavailable at the size required, cut-off times missed across time zones, beneficiary details that fail validation at the last hop, and reference data lost between intermediaries.

5. The last mile

How does value reach the beneficiary’s account domestically, through which licensed institution, on which rails, within which cut-off? Corridor propositions fail here more often than at settlement, and the last mile is the part that cannot be built remotely.

6. The regulatory perimeter

Which activities on this corridor require authorisation, in which market, and who holds it? Which reporting obligations attach, and to whom? A corridor design that is elegant and unlicensable is not a design.

What the data will and will not tell you

Data availability for corridor analysis. Being explicit about the limits is part of the method.
QuestionData qualityCaution
Cost of sending personal remittancesReasonable. The World Bank’s Remittance Prices Worldwide publishes corridor-level pricing quarterly.It covers remittance products, not business payments. Applying it to B2B flows overstates or understates depending on size band.
Value of recorded bilateral tradeReasonable. National statistics and UN Comtrade give recorded merchandise trade.Recorded trade is not payment volume: netting, financing, informal channels and re-exports all break the link.
Value of business cross-border payments on a corridorPoor. Rarely published at corridor level.Most published figures are estimates built on assumptions that are seldom stated. Treat as modelling, not measurement.
Time to settlePoor. Varies by route, size, currency, time of day and compliance outcome.Any single “X days” claim about an entire network is a generalisation. Distributions matter more than averages.
Informal and unrecorded flowsVery poor. By definition unmeasured.Estimates vary by multiples between sources. Cite the source and the method, or do not use the number.

Eko Infrastructure does not publish market-size headlines without a transparent calculation. Where we model, we show the inputs, the assumptions and the arithmetic, and label the result an estimate.

On corridor superlatives

Claims that a particular corridor is the largest of its kind, or carries some multiple of another, are common and are usually unsourced. Where we make a comparative claim we cite the dataset, the year and the definition being used — because “largest” changes answer depending on whether you count value, volume, recorded flows or estimates.
