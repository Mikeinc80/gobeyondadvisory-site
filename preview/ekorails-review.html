The problem

Moving value across African trade corridors is still slower, costlier and harder to reconcile than it should be.

Existing infrastructure is improving. Regional systems, domestic switches and bank networks have all advanced in the last decade. What remains difficult is the specific, corridor-level work of getting a business payment validated, converted, settled and evidenced end to end.

01 — Cost

Costs remain high in many Sub-Saharan corridors

The average cost of sending money to and within Sub-Saharan Africa remains among the highest of any region measured by the World Bank’s Remittance Prices Worldwide database, and business payments carry their own layered bank, correspondent and conversion charges. Corridor-level costs vary widely, and any figure EKORails publishes will be cited and dated. [INSERT VERIFIED FIGURE — corridor cost baseline]

02 — FX availability

Currency access, not just currency price

For many businesses the binding constraint is not the quoted rate but whether the currency is available at the size and time required. Queuing for allocation, splitting orders across providers and holding balances defensively all impose real working-capital costs that do not appear in a headline spread.

03 — Reconciliation

Payments arrive without the data that explains them

When a payment crosses several intermediaries, the invoice reference, the purchase order and the remittance detail frequently do not survive the journey. Finance teams then reconstruct by hand what the payment was for, which delays release of goods and inflates the true cost of the transaction well beyond the fee charged.

04 — Compliance load

Duplicated checks at every hop

Each institution in a chain runs its own screening and due diligence, often on partial information. The result is repeated work, avoidable false positives, payments held for review and a support burden that falls on the customer rather than the chain.

05 — Last mile

Final payout depends on local reach

Settlement between institutions is only part of the problem. Reaching the counterparty’s account at a domestic bank, on a domestic switch, within domestic cut-off times, requires a licensed local partner in every market. Reach is earned market by market.

06 — Fragmentation

Systems that do not yet speak to one another

Regional and domestic systems each solve part of the problem well. The gap that persists is interoperability: connecting a specific pair of markets, with a specific pair of institutions, under a specific set of rules, and evidencing the whole flow to a regulator’s standard.

What EKORails is building

Six capabilities, built in the order a regulated transaction actually needs them.

Each capability below is described exactly as it appears in EKORails LTD’s regulatory filing and supporting technical documentation. Nothing here is offered as a live service. Status labels are applied consistently across this website.

In development

Transaction orchestration

A single instruction model that sequences validation, authorisation, conversion, settlement and confirmation across the parties to a transaction, and holds the state of that transaction until every leg is either complete or unwound.

In development

Compliance and identity controls

Verification of the originating and receiving parties, sanctions and watchlist screening, risk scoring and the transmission of required originator and beneficiary information with each instruction, so that partners receive complete data rather than partial data.

Proposed

FX and liquidity coordination

Coordination with licensed banks, authorised dealers and liquidity providers so that the conversion leg of a transaction is quoted, reserved and executed by an appropriately licensed counterparty. EKORails does not propose to act as a principal in currency conversion.

Proposed

Corridor routing

Selection of an approved route for a given corridor, currency pair and value band from the set of partners that are licensed and operationally available for that route, with the selection recorded and reportable.

In development

Settlement confirmation

Confirmation that the settlement leg has been completed by the licensed partner responsible for it, matched to the original instruction, and returned to both sides with the reference data needed to reconcile against an invoice or purchase order.

In development

Audit and regulatory reporting

An immutable, time-stamped record of every state change in a transaction, retained under a defined schedule, and reporting formats designed to meet the requirements agreed with the relevant regulator and partners for any approved pilot.

How to read our status labels

In development means the component is being built and tested in a non-production environment. Proposed means the design is documented and filed but implementation depends on partner or regulatory outcomes. Nothing on this website is described as live, in production, or carrying real customer value. The same labels are used on the Platform and Technology pages.

Initial use cases

Designed for institutional and business flows, within an approved pilot scope.

The customer groups and transaction types that would be included in any pilot are limited to those set out in EKORails LTD’s regulatory filing and agreed with partners. EKORails does not promote unrestricted retail remittance.

Use case 01

B2B trade invoice settlement

An importer settles a supplier invoice across the corridor with the invoice reference carried through to the beneficiary’s bank statement, so that goods release is not delayed by manual matching.

  • Verified business counterparties on both sides
  • Invoice and purchase-order references preserved end to end
  • Settlement executed by licensed partners

Use case 02

Institutional treasury movement

A corporate or institutional treasury moves funds between its own accounts across the corridor on a scheduled basis, with predictable timing windows and a single reconciled record of each movement.

  • Same-legal-entity transfers within agreed limits
  • Scheduled and evidenced cut-off handling
  • Position and status reporting to the treasury team

Use case 03

Approved supplier and merchant payments

A business pays a defined list of onboarded suppliers or merchants, where each beneficiary has been verified in advance and the payment relationship is recurring rather than ad hoc.

  • Beneficiary whitelisting and pre-verification
  • Velocity and value limits per relationship
  • Batch instruction with individual confirmations

Use case 04

Regulated partner settlement

A licensed bank or payment institution uses the platform to settle its own corridor obligations with a counterparty institution, with the reconciliation and reporting that its own supervisor expects.

  • Institution-to-institution flows
  • Netting and position visibility [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS]
  • Supervisory reporting extracts

Corridor reconciliation and reporting

Participants receive a single, consistent view of every transaction on the corridor — instruction, status, timing, parties, fees and outcome — rather than assembling that view from several partial statements.

  • Per-transaction audit trail
  • Exception and repair queues
  • Scheduled extracts for finance and compliance

How a transaction would work

Six steps — and a clear line between what EKORails does and what licensed partners do.

The description below is a design, not a live service. It is shown so that banks, regulators and enterprise reviewers can see exactly where responsibility sits at every step.

Initiation

An onboarded institution or business submits a payment instruction through an API or a secure portal, carrying the payer, the beneficiary, the amount, the currency pair, the corridor and the commercial reference for the payment.

EKORails: instruction capture, validation, state Partner: originating account relationship

Identity and compliance validation

The parties are checked against the verification records held for them, screened against applicable sanctions and watchlists, and scored against the corridor’s risk rules. Instructions that fail or require review are held, not routed.

EKORails: screening orchestration, risk rules, case creation Partner: regulated customer due diligence, final AML decisioning

Transaction authorisation

The instruction is authorised against the limits, velocity rules and approval workflows agreed for that participant, and the funding leg is confirmed with the institution that holds the customer’s funds.

EKORails: limit and workflow enforcement Partner: funds confirmation and debit authority

FX or value conversion

Where the corridor requires a currency conversion, that conversion is quoted and executed by a licensed bank, authorised dealer or liquidity partner. EKORails records the rate, the timestamp, the executing party and the resulting amounts.

EKORails: quote request, rate capture, record Partner: execution as principal, rate provision, licensing

Partner settlement

The settlement leg is completed by the licensed institution responsible for the receiving market, through the settlement mechanism agreed for the corridor. Customer funds are held by licensed institutions, not by EKORails.

EKORails: instruction, status tracking, exception handling Partner: custody of funds, settlement finality, local payout

Confirmation and audit reporting

Both sides receive confirmation matched to the original commercial reference, and the full sequence of states is written to an append-only audit record available for reconciliation, supervisory reporting and dispute handling.

EKORails: confirmation, audit record, reporting extracts Partner: statement entry, regulatory filings of record

Where the boundary sits

EKORails is being designed as an orchestration, compliance and record-keeping layer. In the model above it does not hold customer funds, does not act as principal in currency conversion, does not provide settlement finality and does not operate a payout licence. Those functions sit with licensed banks, payment institutions, custodians and liquidity partners.

Compliance by design

The control framework is the product, not a layer added after it.

The controls below are the framework EKORails LTD is building and would operate under an approved pilot. EKORails LTD does not hold any compliance or security certification, and does not claim one. Where an audit or certification is completed, it will be named and dated.

Customer and business verification

Identity and entity verification, beneficial ownership, and documented onboarding for every participating institution and business.

AML and CFT controls

A written programme covering risk assessment, policies, training, independent testing and a named compliance officer.

Sanctions screening

Screening of parties and payment data against applicable lists at onboarding and at transaction time, with recorded outcomes.

Transaction monitoring

Rules and thresholds applied to corridor activity, with alerts routed into a case-managed queue rather than cleared automatically.

Limits and velocity controls

Per-participant, per-beneficiary and per-corridor value and frequency caps, enforced before an instruction is routed.

Suspicious activity escalation

Defined escalation paths and timelines to the responsible compliance officer and, where required, to the relevant authority through the licensed partner of record.

Data protection

Lawful basis, minimisation, retention schedules and cross-border transfer controls under the Nigeria Data Protection Act and other applicable regimes.

Cybersecurity

Access control, encryption in transit and at rest, segregation of environments, logging, vulnerability management and independent testing.

Audit trails and reporting

Append-only records of every state change, retained to schedule, with extracts formatted for supervisory and partner reporting.

Platform

A coordination layer for regulated cross-border transactions.

EKORails is being designed to sit between the institutions that already hold licences, funds and market access — orchestrating the sequence, enforcing the controls and holding the record, without taking on the functions that licensed institutions are there to perform.

▲ The platform is in development. No component described on this page is operating in production or carrying customer value. Component status labels are applied consistently throughout.

Platform overview

A cross-border business payment is not one action. It is a sequence of dependent actions performed by different institutions under different rules, where any step can fail and the whole must still be explainable afterwards.

Most of the difficulty in a corridor is not the movement of value itself. It is holding that sequence together: knowing which party is responsible for the current step, what data they need, what they returned, what the customer should be told, and what the supervisor will later expect to see.

EKORails is being built as that coordination layer. It orchestrates the steps, enforces the agreed controls before an instruction is routed, and writes an append-only record of every state change. Funds, conversion, settlement finality and payout remain with licensed institutions.

API and messaging layer

The interface is designed around a small number of resources — participants, beneficiaries, instructions, quotes, transactions, cases and reports — with idempotent submission so that a retried instruction cannot create a duplicate payment.

Message content is aligned to ISO 20022 semantics so that the data a partner bank needs arrives in the form its systems already expect, rather than in a proprietary shape that has to be mapped by hand. [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS — message standard as filed]

Status is delivered by signed webhook with a polling fallback, because a partner that misses a callback must still be able to establish the truth of a transaction independently.

Ledger and record-keeping approach

EKORails maintains an append-only record of transaction state. Entries are written once, never edited; a correction is a new entry that references the entry it corrects, so the history of what was known and when remains intact.

This record is an operational and evidential ledger, not a store of customer value. Balances of customer funds are held and reported by the licensed institutions that hold them.

Retention periods follow the longest applicable requirement across the markets in the corridor. [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS — retention schedule]

Compliance controls

Controls are enforced before routing, not reported after the fact. An instruction that breaches a limit, fails screening or lacks required party data does not proceed to a partner; it becomes a case with an owner and a clock.

The full framework — governance, AML and CFT, KYC and KYB, sanctions, monitoring, fraud, data protection, cybersecurity, resilience, third-party risk, retention and complaints — is set out on the Compliance and Risk page.

Reporting

Three reporting audiences, three different products: operational reporting for participants (status, exceptions, repair queues); financial reporting for finance teams (matched transactions, fees, timing, reconciliation breaks); and supervisory reporting in the formats and at the frequency agreed with the regulator for any approved pilot.

Resilience and business continuity

The design assumption is that partners and networks will be unavailable at some point, and that a payment stuck between two institutions is worse than a payment that was never accepted.

  • Defined recovery time and recovery point objectives per component. [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS — RTO/RPO targets]
  • Degraded-mode behaviour: stop accepting new instructions for an affected route rather than accept and hold indefinitely.
  • Reconciliation-first recovery: establish the true state with each partner before resuming.
  • Tested restore procedures and a documented incident and communications plan.
  • An exit and wind-down plan so that participants are not stranded if the pilot ends.

Insolvency and wind-down

Because EKORails does not hold customer funds, an insolvency of EKORails LTD does not put customer money at risk in the design: funds sit with licensed institutions throughout, and the claim a customer holds is against that institution under its own regime, not against EKORails.

What an EKORails failure would put at risk is coordination and record-keeping. The wind-down plan therefore covers three obligations: stop accepting new instructions immediately; drive every in-flight transaction to a terminal state — settled, returned or rejected — with each partner; and hand participants and their supervisors a complete, readable export of the audit record for the full retention period.

The reverse direction matters equally. If a settlement, conversion or payout partner fails, the platform must be able to establish the true state of every transaction on that route by reconciliation rather than assumption, and to stop routing to it without stranding work already accepted. Concentration on a single partner for any critical function is treated as a risk in its own right.

Use cases

Five flows, each one a real reconciliation problem before it is a payment problem.

The use cases below are the ones set out in EKORails LTD’s regulatory filing. Any pilot would be limited to the customer groups, transaction types and value bands agreed with the regulator and with partners.

In scope

What the platform is being designed to carry.

01 — B2B trade invoice settlement

The situation. An importer in one market owes a supplier in the other against a specific invoice. The payment eventually arrives, but the invoice number does not travel with it. The supplier’s finance team cannot match the credit to the invoice, so goods release, credit terms and the next order all wait on an email chain.

What EKORails is designed to do. Carry the commercial reference — invoice number, purchase order, and agreed remittance data — as structured fields through every leg, so the confirmation the beneficiary receives already identifies what was paid and why.

What partners do. The originating bank debits the payer. A licensed counterparty performs any conversion. A licensed institution in the receiving market credits the beneficiary.

Controls. Both businesses verified before first use; beneficiary pre-registration; per-relationship value and velocity limits; screening on every instruction.

Status. In development [SUBJECT TO REGULATORY APPROVAL]

02 — Institutional treasury movement

The situation. A corporate or institutional treasury needs to move its own funds across the corridor on a predictable schedule. The difficulty is rarely the transfer itself; it is not knowing, at the point of instruction, when the value will land and at what rate, and therefore having to hold defensive balances on both sides.

What EKORails is designed to do. Give the treasury team a single instruction, a recorded rate and executing party, a stated timing window with the corridor’s cut-offs made explicit, and one reconciled record per movement.

What partners do. Hold the accounts on both sides, execute conversion, provide settlement.

Controls. Same-legal-entity verification, dual approval workflow, scheduled limits, full audit trail.

Status. In development

03 — Approved supplier and merchant payments

The situation. A business pays the same set of suppliers or merchants every month. Each payment is screened as though it were the first, which produces repeated verification work and avoidable holds on relationships that are already well understood.

What EKORails is designed to do. Support a whitelisted beneficiary model: each beneficiary verified and registered once, with per-relationship limits, so that recurring payments are screened proportionately and exceptions stand out instead of being buried.

What partners do. Own the regulated due diligence decision on each beneficiary and perform the payout.

Controls. Beneficiary re-verification on a defined cycle; change-of-detail controls to resist invoice redirection fraud; batch submission with individual results.

Status. Proposed

04 — Regulated partner settlement

The situation. Two licensed institutions with corridor obligations to each other reconcile positions across statements, spreadsheets and email, and discover breaks days after the fact.

What EKORails is designed to do. Provide both institutions with the same transaction record in the same form at the same time, with exceptions raised as cases rather than discovered in a month-end reconciliation.

What partners do. Everything that requires a licence: funds, conversion, settlement and the filings of record with their own supervisors.

Controls. Institution-level access control, segregation of duties, supervisory reporting extracts, netting arrangements where permitted [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS].

Status. Proposed

05 — Corridor reconciliation and reporting

The situation. No participant has a complete view of the corridor. Each holds a fragment, and assembling the whole is manual work performed after something has already gone wrong.

What EKORails is designed to do. Maintain one authoritative record per transaction — instruction, parties, states, timings, executing partners, fees and outcome — available as scheduled extracts to finance, operations and compliance teams.

What partners do. Confirm their legs; their own books remain their records of account.

Controls. Append-only audit record, role-based extract permissions, retention to schedule.

Status. In development

What a sandbox is

A supervised test with limits — not permission to operate.

What sandbox participation would mean

What sandbox participation would not mean

KYC and KYB

Every participating institution and business is verified before it can transact: legal identity, registration, regulatory status where applicable, beneficial ownership, directors and authorised signatories, and the nature and expected pattern of its corridor activity.

Individuals acting for a participant are identity-verified and their authority evidenced. Verification is refreshed on a risk-based cycle, and material changes — ownership, control, banking details — trigger re-verification rather than a note on file.

Evidence is retained in a form that a partner or supervisor can inspect.

Sanctions

Parties and payment data are screened at onboarding and at transaction time against applicable sanctions and watchlists for both markets in the corridor, including lists that partners are separately obliged to apply.

Matches are not auto-cleared. A potential match holds the instruction, creates a case with an owner and a service level, and is dispositioned by a person whose decision is recorded with its rationale. List coverage and final disposition responsibility sit with the licensed partner of record.

Transaction monitoring

Corridor activity is monitored against rules and thresholds calibrated to the expected pattern for each participant: value, frequency, counterparties, timing, structuring indicators and deviation from a participant’s own baseline.

Alerts route to a case-managed queue with ownership and timelines. Rules are tuned on evidence, and every change to a rule is versioned so that a supervisor can see what was being monitored on any given date.

EKORails does not claim that monitoring detects all illicit activity. No monitoring programme can, and any statement to the contrary would be false.

Fraud prevention

Controls target the fraud types that actually affect corridor payments: invoice redirection and beneficiary-detail changes, business email compromise, account takeover of a participant’s portal user, and mule beneficiary accounts.

Measures include change-of-detail verification through a separate channel, beneficiary whitelisting, dual approval above defined thresholds, device and session controls, and anomaly detection on instruction patterns.

Suspicious activity escalation

Defined escalation paths and timelines from the analyst to the named compliance officer, and from the compliance officer to the relevant authority through the licensed partner of record where the reporting obligation sits with that partner.

Tipping-off restrictions are respected: escalation procedures are designed so that a participant is not told anything that would prejudice an investigation.

Processing is carried out on a defined lawful basis, minimised to what the transaction and applicable rules require, and retained to a published schedule. Data subject rights — access, rectification, erasure where applicable, objection and portability — are handled through a documented process with a response deadline.

Technology

Engineered for evidence, not for demonstration.

Payment infrastructure is judged on what it can prove after something goes wrong. This page describes how EKORails is being built, at the level of detail that is useful to a reviewer without weakening the controls it describes.

▲ Architecture is in development. Nothing described here is running in production or carrying customer value. Specific security configuration is shared with partners and reviewers under NDA rather than published.

Engineering principles

Six commitments that shape every decision below.

Principle 01

One state, always knowable

A transaction is in exactly one state, and that state can be established independently by every party to it. Ambiguity is treated as an incident.

Principle 02

Idempotent by default

A retried instruction never creates a second payment. Every write path is safe to repeat, because in a distributed corridor it will be repeated.

Principle 03

Controls before routing

Limits, screening and data completeness are enforced before an instruction reaches a partner, not reconciled afterwards.

Principle 04

Append-only history

Records are written once. Corrections reference what they correct, so the history of what was known, and when, survives.

Principle 05

Fail closed

When a dependency is unavailable or a control cannot be evaluated, the instruction stops. Accepting work that cannot complete is the worse failure.

Principle 06

Least privilege, least data

Access is granted narrowly and reviewed; data is collected only where a rule or the transaction requires it.

Data model

A small number of long-lived entities — participant, user, beneficiary, instruction, quote, transaction, case, report — with explicit relationships and no implicit shared state between services. Money amounts are stored as integer minor units with an explicit currency, never as floating point.

Every record carries the identity of the actor that created it, the time it was created and, where applicable, the rule version that was in force. That is what makes a supervisory question about a past date answerable.

Security posture

Authentication between institutions is mutual and certificate-based; human access to operational systems requires multi-factor authentication and is granted by role, reviewed on a defined cycle and revoked on leaving. Production access is not routine and is logged.

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest with managed keys and a documented rotation policy. Environments are segregated, and production data is not used in development or test. Dependencies are inventoried and patched to a defined schedule; secrets are managed centrally and never held in source control.

Independent penetration testing is performed before any pilot and on a defined cycle thereafter, with findings tracked to closure. Provider, scope and dates: [CONFIRM WITH EKORAILS]

Specific configurations, network topology, control thresholds and vendor names are not published on this website, because publishing them would help an attacker more than a reviewer. They are provided to partners, auditors and supervisors under NDA.

Environments and testing

Separate development, test and production environments, with change moving in one direction through peer review and automated checks. Test coverage focuses where the risk is: state transitions, idempotency, control enforcement, partner failure modes and reconciliation.

A partner sandbox with representative fixtures is planned so that an integrating institution can test failure paths — not only the path where everything works.

Observability

Structured logs, metrics and distributed tracing across the transaction lifecycle, with alerts defined on the things that matter operationally: stuck transactions, control-engine failures, partner latency and error rates, screening backlogs and reconciliation breaks.

Logs exclude sensitive payload content by design, and access to operational telemetry is itself access-controlled.

Institutional partnerships

Built with institutions, or not built at all.

Every function in a corridor that requires a licence is performed by an institution that holds one. That makes partnership the whole strategy rather than a channel within it.

▲ This page is for institutional, enterprise, technology, regulatory and research partnerships only. EKORails does not solicit investment through this website, does not publish investment terms and does not accept investment enquiries through this form.

Partner categories

Seven kinds of partner, each with a different first conversation.

Banks and licensed payment institutions

Institutions that can hold customer funds, execute conversion, provide settlement or effect payout in either market of the corridor. First conversation: scope, licensing, control expectations, data sharing and the split of regulated responsibilities.

Enterprise and trade partners

Businesses with recurring, documented corridor obligations who are willing to participate in a controlled pilot within the approved scope. First conversation: your actual flows, your current reconciliation problem and whether it is in scope.

FX and liquidity providers

Licensed counterparties able to quote and execute the conversion leg at the sizes and times the corridor requires. First conversation: currency pairs, size bands, quote mechanics and settlement arrangements.

Technology and cybersecurity providers

Infrastructure, resilience, testing and assurance partners supporting a platform intended for institutional use. First conversation: scope of engagement, security requirements and subcontracting.

Identity and compliance providers

Verification, screening, monitoring and case-management capabilities meeting institutional standards in both markets of a corridor. First conversation: coverage, list sources, latency, accuracy and evidence retention.

Regulators and policy institutions

Supervisors, policy bodies, development finance institutions and trade organisations engaging on interoperability, corridor economics, controls and market structure. Enquiries route directly to the named compliance officer.

Research and development partners

Institutions collaborating on corridor data, settlement research and market analysis, published through Eko Infrastructure with sources and named authors.
